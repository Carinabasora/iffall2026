Creative Brief

Carina Basora

11/2/2026

Project Title: The Luna Moth

1. Project Overview

The goal of my website is to introduce viewers to the luna moth: what it looks like, how it grows and lives, and why it is so rarely seen even though it isn't actually rare. The site should be informational but led by imagery, so that the photography and illustration do most of the storytelling and the text supports them.

2. Resources

. Photography of the moth's life stages, wings, eyespots, and antennae is available through the article and Wikimedia Commons. Botanical drawings of the host trees (walnut, hickory, sweetgum, and white birch) can be found from public-domain collections.

3. Audience

The site is intended for all ages. It is an introduction to the topic for people who may never have seen a luna moth, so the content should be easy to scan, clearly written, and not too technical. Because it is part of my portfolio, instructors and prospective employers may also review it.

4. Message

The luna moth is one of the most interesting insects in North America, yet most people have never seen one because the adults only live for about a week, fly at night, and never eat. I want viewers to leave knowing a little more about these moths and appreciating how much happens in such a short life.

5. Tone

Calm and not overwhelming. The site should feel quiet and unhurried, with interesting imagery that invites people to slow down and look. Captions and text should be short, plain, and friendly.

6. Visual Style

I'd like to leave the final look to the designer's interpretation, within this direction:

  • Imagery-forward: large photographs, gallery-style layouts, and small captions. Let the images lead and keep text minimal.
  • Color: muted colors drawn from the moth itself, such as pale green, off-white, and soft browns, with the deep maroon-brown of the wing edge as a possible accent. No harsh or saturated colors.
  • Clean, modern, and organic: generous white space and simple layouts, with natural touches such as soft curves or leaf shapes rather than hard boxes.
  • Botanical drawings: use illustrations of host trees and leaves as accents and section details, especially on the Habitat and Diet page.
  • Typography: friendly, highly readable type. A refined serif for headings paired with a clean sans-serif for body text would work well.

7. Site Structure and Content

Six pages with simple, consistent navigation across the top:

  • Home: large hero image and a short introduction to the moth.
  • Appearance: wings, eyespots, tails, size, and differences between males and females, in a photo gallery.
  • Life Cycle: the main feature. Egg, caterpillar, cocoon, and adult as a visual step-by-step sequence with short captions.
  • Habitat and Diet: range across eastern North America and host trees, with botanical drawings.
  • Defenses and Threats: how the moth avoids predators and what harms its population.
  • Sources: Wikipedia article, image credits, and other references.

8. Practical Details

  • The site should be responsive and work well on both desktop and mobile.
  • Text should have strong contrast against backgrounds, and images need alt text.
  • Images should be optimized so pages load quickly.
  • Each image is credited on the Sources page.

9. Designer's Creative Input

The designer will have full creative autonomy of the final color palette, font pairing, and layout for each page, and to suggest ways to present the life cycle sequence in a way that is inspired by the luna moth. Any ideas for how the botanical drawings and photography work together are welcome:)
